# Synthesis

Merge of chunk reports 01–04 (`review/METHOD.md` §3). Each claim cites the finding it rests on: **01-F3** means report 01, finding 3. The build-process review from earlier the same day (D-023) is cited as **D-023**.

The four agents are all drawn from one model family, and so is the orchestrator. Where they agree, that counts for more only when they reached the point from different questions. It is weak evidence where they shared a premise.

## Verdict in one paragraph

The program's core design holds. Carrying the arc in escalating cases and the concepts in modules is sound (02-F7). The form suits a reader working alone better than expected (04-F6). The guard against self-justification is stronger than a first-principles design would have produced (01-F6). What the review finds is not a wrong design but a program built from inside one picture of its reader. That reader is a single office professional who reasons with argumentative texts, has library access, and has already noticed that something is wrong. Nothing has yet tested that picture against anyone else (01-F1, 01-F2). Most findings are consequences of it, and most of the fixes are cheap because Modules 4 to 8 are unwritten.

## Where the chunks converge

### 1. Nobody outside the drafting process has read it, and nothing says what reading it should achieve
Three reports converge here from different questions: 01-F1, 01-F2, 04-R5 and D-023. The completion standard tests inputs (sources, locators, quotations), not what a reader can do afterwards. The only reader test planned is Robert's read-through of the finished program. The charter itself names the cost: an error in the form "would be repeated across every module before anyone noticed." This is the strongest convergence in the review.

### 2. The reader named is broader than the reader written for
01-F3 and 01-F4 find that the program is written for a graduate office professional with library access. The charter's church, school and community organisation appear nowhere in the modules or cases. Two other reports find the same narrowing from other directions. 02-F3 finds that every response is an individual's, and that collective and organised action is absent. 03-F1 finds that every reading is a text that argues: there is no testimony, history or literature. No decision records any of these narrowings (01-F3, 02-F2, 03-F1). They look inherited rather than chosen.

### 3. Drift: placed last by the architecture, excluded by the sourcing rule
This convergence was not visible from any single chunk.

- **The architecture.** 02-F4 finds that the charter's own example, "a culture that slowly shifts what everyone treats as normal", has its only home in Module 8, the last module. The step of noticing that something is wrong has no home at all, because Module 1 begins after a demand is already believed wrong.
- **The literature.** 03-F1 and 03-F2 find that the literature best placed to explain drift, the behavioural and organisational research on ethical fading and on normalised deviance, is entirely absent. None of the 20 empirical works mapped passes the free-text rule.

The architecture and the sourcing rule reinforce each other's blind spot: the part of the problem the program reaches last is the part its rules keep it from equipping. For many readers drift is how the situation arrives, which makes it arguably the central experience of the charter's reader.

### 4. The positions record is both the reader's tool and the program's test
04-F4 and 04-F5 find that comparing one's positions across modules is "part of the point", yet it rests on memory alone. Module 3's case even tells the reader that the remembered version "has been improving". 01-F9 finds that the same before-and-after structure is the cheapest available measure of whether the program changes anything. One page would serve both purposes (04-R2, 01-R1).

## Where the chunks conflict: questions for Robert

**A. Access versus completion.** 03-F3 finds that principle 9 has hardened in practice from "prefer" into a ratchet. It proposes a budget of one library-only text per module, which would readmit work such as Havel and Weber (03-R2). But 04-F9 identifies Module 1's first library-only text as the likeliest place a reader stops. 01-F3 counts library access as part of what narrows the reader. Both readings of the evidence are reasonable. Which cost the program prefers to pay is an editorial decision.

**B. Completeness versus load.** 02-R1 wants those harmed on the core path, because Modules 2 and 3 both flag the gap and nothing fills it (02-F1). No reason is recorded for making it optional (02-F2). But 04-F2 puts the program at 45 to 55 hours already. Of 02's three options, option (b), which gives an unwritten Module 5 or 7 an outward-facing brief, adds the least load. Option (a), a ninth module, adds the most.

**C. When to test on readers.** Reports 01 and 04 both recommend a pilot of Module 1 now (01-R1, 04-R5). D-006 deliberately held the reader pass back until the program is finished. The reports do not claim D-006 was wrong. They claim the pilot tests the form, and that the D-006 pass tests the sequence; these are different tests. This needs Robert's decision because it revisits a recorded one.

**D. Literature.** 03-F4 finds that nine free, chapter-locatable literary works have never been considered. 04 would expect them to strain a form built around argument, and to add load. The review recommends only that the question be decided and recorded, not that fiction be added.

## Recommendations

Tiered by what they need. Nothing here has been applied.

### Tier 1: cheap, no change to the architecture. Could be done now with Robert's approval.
1. **State the time** each module and the whole program take (04-R1). One line each, after an honest recount of the primary texts.
2. **Add a one-page positions record** the reader can copy, and have each closing quote back the earlier module's options (04-R2; convergence 4). Writing stays optional.
3. **Mark one question per reading** as the one to answer if the reader answers nothing else (04-R3). This ranks questions, not readings, so the no-pre-judging rule is not breached.
4. **Add a plain signpost for a reader in difficulty now:** the *kinds* of help available, naming no body that has not been checked (01-R3, 01-F5).
5. **Add a check for time and commentary-to-text ratio** to CHECKLIST §D (04-R4).
6. **Record why Kutz (2000) and Lepora and Goodin (2013) are not in Module 3** (03-R7, 03-F6).

### Tier 2: structural. Robert decides.
7. **Pilot Module 1 now** with five to eight readers across the charter's settings. Use an unseen before-and-after case, and record timing and where readers stop (01-R1, 04-R5; convergence 1; conflict C).
8. **State four to six program outcomes in CHARTER,** naming capacities rather than conclusions (01-R2).
9. **Move drift and noticing earlier.** Place A.2 ("It worked") in Module 4 or 5, and give noticing a home. Module 8 keeps endurance and aftermath (02-R3; convergence 3).
10. **Decide whether the program teaches any empirical evidence.** If it does, the cheapest vehicle is a short sourced paragraph per module, held to the pointer standard (03-R1; convergence 3). This is the recommendation most likely to change what the program is.
11. **Put those harmed on the core path**, choosing among 02's options (02-R1; conflict B).
12. **Write collective action into the briefs for Modules 5 and 6** (02-R2; convergence 2).
13. **Decide the reader question explicitly:** either narrow the charter to the reader the program serves, or widen the cases and add a prompt per module to put the reader's own situation in place of the case's (01-R4; convergence 2).
14. **Restate principle 9 as a budget or keep it strict** (03-R2; conflict A).

### Tier 3: carry into drafting Modules 4 to 8.
15. **Write Case C before Module 4** (02-R4). This is already an open question.
16. **Test the principle 8 candidates as each module is drafted.** They are *Shabbat* 54b–55a (Module 4), Mencius 5B1 with *Analects* 18.1 (Module 6), *Zhuangzi* ch. 4 (Module 6 or 8), and Bhishma Parva §43 with Ghazali, *Iḥyāʾ* Book 14 (Module 7) (03-R3). All are low-to-medium confidence and none has been passage-tested; the project has made and withdrawn a claim of this kind before, in two forms (D-010). Robert must also decide whether the Talmud counts as outside the Western canon (03-F7).
17. **Draw historical cases from free public-inquiry reports** (TRC vol. 4, Francis 2015, Horizon), including the cases principle 7 promises of people who stayed and were right (03-R4).

## What this review did not do

- It did not test anything on a reader. Every judgment about readers is a model's prediction.
- The primary-text lengths and hours in 04 are estimates, because the source sites were blocked for that agent.
- The literature map in 03 is largely from memory. Items not marked (w) are unverified, and Sefaria and ctext.org were blocked.
- No candidate reading was put through CHECKLIST §B.
