# 02 — Problem map

## Summary

1. The program has two axes: modules carry concepts, and escalating cases carry the arc. That pairing is sound. Re-carving is not warranted.
2. Those harmed are the clearest gap. Modules 2 and 3 each flag it as "accumulating". Yet E3 is optional, and neither ARCHITECTURE nor D-001 says why. A "complete" program would leave the gap open permanently.
3. Nothing in the architecture covers collective or organised action. Every option in Module 6 is an individual's.
4. Drift and habituation are the charter's own fourth example, but their only home is Module 8, the last. Noticing has no home.
5. E1 as an expansion is defensible, because Modules 1, 2 and 6 absorb much of it. E3 as an expansion is not.

## Question

What are the parts of the problem of acting inside a compromised system, and in what order does a person meet them?

## First principles

*Written from 00-problem.md alone, before any other program file was opened.*

**Parts.** Thirteen distinct questions, plus three cross-cutting dimensions.

1. **Noticing.** Is anything wrong here? Normalisation, euphemism and a partial view make wrongs invisible from inside.
2. **Diagnosis.** What kind of wrong, how deep, whose doing, and is it reformable? Dysfunction, injustice and corruption call for different things.
3. **Drift.** How did I (and people like me) come to take part without deciding to? Incremental commitment, role socialisation, obedience.
4. **My share.** How am I connected to the wrong: doing, enabling, benefiting, inheriting, keeping silent? How much of it is mine?
5. **Am I right?** Moral uncertainty, disagreement with colleagues, the case where the institution is right and I am wrong, and the vices of the objector (self-righteousness, clean-hands vanity).
6. **Role and loyalty.** What do I owe the institution, the role and colleagues, and when does that give way?
7. **Those harmed.** What is owed to the people the institution wrongs: acknowledgement, protection, testimony, redress?
8. **Repertoire.** What can I actually do: comply, work within, quiet non-cooperation, voice, refusal, whistleblowing, exit? And the insider's argument ("if I go, someone worse replaces me").
9. **Cost and demand.** How much can morality ask of me, given dependants and my own dependence?
10. **Acting with others.** Allies, organising, collective refusal; the coordination problem that makes solitary conscience weak.
11. **Staying well.** Living inside for years: moral injury, cynicism, keeping a self that can still see.
12. **Aftermath.** Guilt, shame, agent-regret, repair, forgiveness for what I have already done.
13. **Building.** What would an institution worth belonging to look like, and what is reform?

Cross-cutting dimensions, not parts: **position** (front line, manager, leader, contractor or citizen), which changes leverage and options in every part; **kind of compromise** (inherited, drifted into, demanded, benefited from, chosen for good ends); and **integrity itself**, the concept every part relies on.

Merged: "judging colleagues who comply" into 5 and 6; "exit" into 8 (the problem says leaving is impractical, which makes it an option to be weighed, not excluded); "dirty hands" (doing wrong for good ends) into 8 and 4; "institution right, objector wrong" into 5.

**Order met.** The order of the world and the order of awareness differ. In the world, drift (3) comes before noticing (1). In awareness, the reader typically meets: unease (1) → sense-making (2, 5) → implication (4, then 3 in retrospect) → obligations (6, 7) → options (8, 9, 10) → living with it (11) → looking back (12) → building (13). The reader named in the problem is *already* implicated and benefiting. So they arrive mid-arc, and parts 4 and 12 (my share, my past) are live from the first page, not at the end.

**Three-plus carvings.**

| Carving | Centre | Margins |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Arc of a situation** (notice → understand → decide → act → endure → look back) | The practical sequence; matches experience | Structural and inherited complicity with no decision point; non-linearity; collective action tends to become one step among many |
| **B. Kind of compromise** (inherited, drifted, demanded, benefited, dirty hands) | The moral structure of implication; different kinds warrant different responses | Repertoire, endurance and collective action are shared across kinds and get repeated or orphaned; real cases mix kinds |
| **C. Capacity built** (perception, judgment, action, endurance, repair) | What the reader can do afterwards, so it is testable | The substantive theory of complicity; victims and relationships; the institution as object |
| **D. Relationship** (to institution, colleagues, those harmed, oneself) | People; gives victims and colleagues a place of their own | Epistemics (am I right?), drift, time |
| **E. Position** (junior, manager, leader, outsider-insider) | Power and leverage; realism about options | The shared conceptual core, which it repeats |
| **F. Concept order** (complicity → responsibility → integrity → dissent → ...) | Conceptual clarity; how philosophy arranges the literature | The reader's temporal experience, perception, social science |

My preferred carving is **A as spine, entered mid-arc**, with C as the outcome check and D as a completeness check (every relationship must be somebody's module). F is the carving most at risk of being adopted by default, because the literature is shelved that way.

**Easy to forget, argued.**

- **Drift: belongs, early.** Most people enter wrongdoing by increments, not decisions. A program that starts at a decision point assumes a reader who does not exist.
- **Noticing: belongs, but is hard to teach by reading.** Cases carry it better than arguments.
- **Collective action: belongs as a core part.** The solitary-conscience frame is philosophy's habit, not the problem's shape. Most effective resistance inside institutions is organised.
- **Those harmed: belongs as core.** Without it, "integrity" collapses into the agent's own cleanliness, which is exactly the objection (moral self-indulgence) the program must meet.
- **Past compromises: belongs, and early.** The reader is described as already implicated.
- **Institution right, objector wrong: belongs as core.** "Rarely certain they are right" is in the problem's own statement.
- **Building/reform: partly.** The reader is not necessarily a reformer, but "better able to act" includes improving what one is inside. It can sit within the repertoire rather than standing alone.
- **Judging others who complied:** easy to forget, and it bears on loyalty and self-judgment alike.

## Comparison with the program

**Mapping.** Part 6 → Modules 1–2. Part 4 is split across Module 3 (another's wrong) and Module 5 (nobody's in particular). The split is defensible, because it tracks kind of compromise. Part 8 → Modules 4 and 6. Part 9 → Module 7. Parts 3, 11 and 12 → Module 8. Part 7 → E3. Part 13 → E1. Part 5 is distributed (principles 3 and 4). Parts 1, 2 and 10 have no home, though Arendt (Module 1) partly touches 1.

**Order.** ARCHITECTURE claims "the arc of a situation rather than the structure of a discipline". Modules 1–5 follow a discipline's order (obligation, role morality, complicity, silence, many hands). Only Modules 6–8 follow the arc. The cases carry the arc instead. A.1 is a junior asked to soften a report, A.4 the same person as manager, and A.6 the manager benefiting from another's fraud. That is carving A, plus position (E), built into the case line. So the design runs concepts on one axis and situation on the other, which is better than either alone. ARCHITECTURE does not describe it that way.

**Reasons for non-modules.** Both reasons hold. Uncertainty is distributed in practice: every module has "Two questions about yourself". Authority and law are folded into Module 1, with E2 as an outlet, which fits the charter's exclusion of a civil-disobedience course.

**Expansions.** E1's justification argues for necessity: "Without it, the reader may finish with no account of why anyone should stay". Much of E1 is absorbed elsewhere, though: the *Crito* and Rawls in Module 1, Module 2, and "staying to repair" in Module 6. E3's only reason is that it is "Distinct from Module 8", which explains why it is separate, not why it is optional.

## Findings

1. **Those harmed are a flagged, unfilled gap, deferred to an optional module.** Module 2's closing says the people on the receiving end are "mostly present here as examples", a gap "accumulating rather than arriving". Module 3's closing calls it "a real gap ... not filled by anything in this module". ARCHITECTURE puts the topic in E3, calls the core path alone "finished, not abandoned", and sends A.3 (harm to an outsider) to E3 as well. *Evidence, high.* That this is a defect: *Judgment, high.* Without it, integrity reads as the agent's own cleanliness.
2. **No reason is recorded for making E3 optional.** Neither ARCHITECTURE (lines 81–82) nor D-001 gives one. *Evidence, high.* The choice looks unexamined rather than considered. *Judgment, medium.*
3. **Collective and organised action is absent** as a topic from ARCHITECTURE, CHARTER and Modules 1–3. Module 6's options are all individual, and ARCHITECTURE line 89 concedes the carving assumes "an individual agent deciding". *Evidence, high.* That this is a missing part of the problem, not a scope choice: *Judgment, medium-high.*
4. **Drift comes last and noticing has no place.** The charter's fourth example is "a culture that slowly shifts what everyone treats as normal". ARCHITECTURE places habituation only in Module 8, and A.2 ("It worked") is pencilled in for Module 8. Module 1 opens after a wrong has been perceived: "A demand arrives that you believe is wrong". *Evidence, high.* That a reader meets drift before deciding, not after: *Judgment, medium.*
5. **Module 8 is overloaded.** It combines drift, staying well over years, and aftermath. My first-principles analysis found these to be three parts met at different times. *Judgment, medium.*
6. **Silence is split from the other responses, although Module 6's own rationale covers it.** Module 6 is one module because the options are "rival uses of the same occasion", and saying nothing in the meeting is one of those uses. *Evidence, high.* That merging is better: *Judgment, low.* Module 4 also covers truth-telling, and Module 3 already hands off to it.
7. **The two-axis design (concepts × escalating case) is sound.** None of my alternative carvings clearly beats it. Switching to carving A would mean re-sequencing Modules 1–3 (about 19,600 words by `wc`) and rebuilding A.1 → A.4 → A.6. *Judgment, medium.*
8. **The case where the objector is wrong is the weak point of distributing uncertainty.** Case C is a placeholder (CASES, OPEN-QUESTIONS). *Evidence, high.*

## Recommendations

1. **Put those harmed on the core path** (F1, F2). The options for Robert are: (a) make E3 core module 9, which moves the completion line; (b) give Module 5 or 7 an outward-facing brief and place A.3 there, which is cheap because both are unwritten, though the gap risks being flagged again rather than filled; or (c) merge Modules 4 and 6 (F6) and use the freed slot, which means editing Module 3's hand-off. Each needs a DECISIONS entry.
2. **Write collective action into Module 6's brief**, and probably Module 5's (F3). Cost: low. They are unwritten. It may need a social-science or labour-history reading, which bears on chunk 03.
3. **Move drift earlier** (F4, F5). Place A.2 in Module 4 or 5, and leave Module 8 with endurance and aftermath. Cost: low. A.2 is unplaced, and Modules 4 and 5 are unwritten.
4. **Write Case C before Module 4** (F8). Cost: none new; it is already an open question.
5. **Restate ARCHITECTURE's organizing logic:** cases carry the arc, modules the concepts (F7). Cost: a wording change and a DECISIONS entry.
6. **Keep E1 as an expansion**, but have Module 6 treat "staying to repair" as a full option. Cost: nil now.
