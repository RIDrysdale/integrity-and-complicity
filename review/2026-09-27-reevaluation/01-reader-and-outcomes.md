# 01 — Reader and outcomes

## Summary

The program states no outcome for its reader and has no way to find out whether it worked. Its completion standard tests inputs, and the only reader test planned is Robert's read-through of the finished program (CHARTER; D-006). Judged by its prose, cases and readings, it is written for a university-educated office professional on a management track who is looking back at a situation. The charter names a broader reader, including church, school and community organisation. Nothing drafted serves those settings or anyone outside white-collar work. The program guards well against the reader looking for justification and against heroism. It says nothing to a reader in crisis about where practical help is. It already asks readers to record a position before reading and compare it afterwards, which could become a cheap test. Top recommendation: pilot Module 1 now with five to eight readers of different kinds.

## Question

Who concretely is this program for, and what should they be able to do after it that they could not before? How would anyone know whether the program achieved that?

## First principles

*Written from `00-problem.md` and `METHOD.md` alone, before any other file was opened. The wording was condensed for length after the comparison. Nothing of substance was changed.*

**Readers.**

- **A. NHS ward manager**, aged 44. She signs off unsafe rotas and is asked to "reword" incident reports. She has a mortgage, children and a pension she cannot move. She has 20–30 tired minutes a night and no philosophy. *Needs:* words for the gap between her professional code and her ward; options short of both silence and a formal case; recognition that what she feels is moral injury; companionship.
- **B. Care worker on an employer-sponsored visa** (not white-collar). The care home falsifies logs, and her right to remain depends on her employer. English is her second language, and she reads on a phone. *Needs:* safety first: what she can do without losing her visa, and where to get confidential advice. Then permission not to be a hero, small low-exposure options, and the consolation that complying under duress is not endorsing.
- **C. Government policy official**, a graduate implementing a policy she thinks unjust, with 3–4 hours a week. *Needs:* understanding of diffuse, authorised complicity; judgment on staying or going; self-knowledge about whether "softening it from within" is true.
- **D. Lay member of a religious community whose leaders concealed abuse**, aged 57. Leaving would cost belonging and identity, not income. *Needs:* resources that take the tradition seriously; what a member without office can do; whether staying is endorsement; consolation.
- **E. Trainee** lawyer, accountant or doctor, not yet compromised. *Needs:* to recognise early drift, and to draw lines in advance.
- **Risk readers:** the reader *in acute crisis* (a disciplinary, retaliation, a decision this week, or ill health), and the reader *seeking justification*.

Only C clearly wants theory for its own sake. B needs practical and safety information that no philosophical text supplies. The readers also differ in time, in what leaving would cost them, and in whether the institution is a workplace at all.

**Outcomes.** Afterwards a reader can:

1. describe their own involvement precisely: doing, directing, enabling, benefiting, failing to prevent;
2. name three or more options between compliance and exit, and who bears the cost of each;
3. recognise standard rationalisations in sample reasoning and in their own;
4. state in advance a line they would not cross;
5. say how they would test whether they are right;
6. name one route to practical help in their sector and jurisdiction;
7. hold their own complicity without denial or paralysis.

"Takes action within N weeks" is deliberately excluded, since it could harm reader B.

**Cheap tests.**

- Five to eight pilot readers across A–E, at least two with no degree.
- One unseen case answered at the start and the end, scored against outcomes 1, 2, 3 and 5.
- Where each reader stops, and how long each unit takes.
- A follow-up at one and three months.
- An adverse-effect question: did anything make you feel worse without helping, or give you an excuse you now distrust?

**Risks.**

1. A reader in crisis gets philosophy when they need an adviser.
2. The readings supply rationalisations.
3. Heroism bias pushes a precarious reader into ruinous action.
4. Guilt without relief deepens moral injury.
5. Class and sector narrowness.
6. Exercises about a real workplace create discoverable documents.
7. Jurisdiction: practical routes differ by country.
8. Isolation: questions written for a group, given to a reader who has none.

## Comparison with the program

**Named reader.** CHARTER, Who it is for: "An adult who has encountered the problem at work, in a profession, in government, in a church, in a school, or in a community organization, and who wants to think about it more carefully than the situation allowed at the time." "At the time" makes this a reader looking back, yet the charter's question is how to *act*, and its concern is "personal conduct".

**Implied reader.** Every placed case is set in one office: an internal report, a quarterly pack, a budget, an analyst and a data extract (CASES A.1, A.4, A.6). Module 1 assigns two texts that need a library (D-004). The apparatus expects close textual work, such as separating four arguments in the *Crito*. The prose is fine and demanding. It suits readers C and E. Reader A can transpose it with effort, and it fails readers B and D.

**Outcomes.** Each module's "By the end you should be able to" concerns understanding the readings. The program states no outcome of its own.

## Findings

1. **Evidence, high.** No program-level outcome is stated anywhere. The CHARTER Standard of completion tests inputs: case, readings, locators, quotations. The module-level "By the end" statements concern the readings' positions, not the reader's situation or conduct. At present nobody could tell whether the program worked.
2. **Evidence, high.** The only reader test planned is Robert reading the finished program (CHARTER, Standard of completion 2; D-006). He is the editor, not the named reader. The charter records the cost: "an error in the form itself would be repeated across every module before anyone noticed."
3. **Judgment, medium–high.** The program is written for a narrower reader than it names: a graduate office professional on a management track, with library access and stamina for textual work. The charter's church, school and community organisation appear nowhere in `modules/` or CASES.md (grep). No DECISIONS entry addresses who the reader is, so the narrowing is unexamined.
4. **Judgment, medium.** The recurring case limits who can use the program, though less than it seems. Reader A can map "soften the report" onto incident reports. Reader B cannot: the case offers "Leave, or begin planning to" (M1, The case) and later hands the reader a budget (A.4), both of which assume a career and an exit that can be planned. Reader D is served better by the readings than by the case. The *Crito*'s argument from having stayed is her question exactly, but the apparatus puts it to "an employer" (M1, Reading 2).
5. **Evidence, high.** The reader-facing files never mention where to get practical help: legal advice, a union, a regulator, a confidential advice service, or health support (grep of `modules/`, README, CHARTER). "Not a whistleblowing manual" (CHARTER) is a sound limit on scope, but it does not require silence about where help lies.
6. **Evidence + Judgment, high.** On the reader seeking justification and on heroism, the program is right, and more rigorous than my sketch. Every module poses both self-scrutiny questions (DESIGN-PRINCIPLES 3). A.6 asks: "Are you drawing the distinctions because they are true, or because you need them to be?" M3 asks: "How might the care I am taking be the way out?" The CHARTER says the program is "Not a program in which the reader is the hero", and M1 asks whether Socrates' refusal was "a cost imposed on people who did not choose it."
7. **Judgment, medium.** Relief is deferred. Excuse, moral remainder and self-forgiveness are placed in Modules 7–8 (ARCHITECTURE), while Modules 1–3 turn suspicion on the reader in both directions. For a distressed reader like A, that is a plausible point to drop out, and a possible harm. Untested.
8. **Evidence, medium.** The exercises are about a second-person fiction. The positions, the "Monday / Thursday / the 12th" actions and the "Two questions about yourself" all concern the case. A few questions reach toward the reader's real job (M1: "What exactly did you accept when you took the job?"). The program never explicitly invites the reader to put their own situation in the case's place. That gives distance and leaves no discoverable records (risk 6), but it costs transfer to the reader's life. It is not recorded as a decision.
9. **Evidence, high.** A before-and-after comparison is already built in. The reader fixes a position before reading, keeps it, and compares it later (M1, The case and What you would actually do on Monday; the M2 and M3 closings). For the program's purposes, though, it measures *change* in judgment, not improvement, and it uses a case the reader has already seen.
10. **Judgment, medium.** Against my outcomes, the drafted modules serve 1 (M3), 3 and 5 strongly, and 2 partly. The case options model alternatives, and Module 6 is planned for them. They do not yet serve 4 or 7 (possibly Module 8), or 6 (not planned anywhere).

## Recommendations

1. **Pilot Module 1 now,** with five to eight readers spread across the settings the charter names. Include at least two readers outside offices and two without degrees. Use an unseen before-and-after case, record where each reader stops and how long they take, and ask the adverse-effect question. *Cost:* recruitment, a one-page case with a scoring sheet, and two to four weeks. This is a structural proposal to Robert. It does not replace the D-006 reader pass, which tests the whole sequence; it tests the form before five more modules repeat it. Rests on F1, F2 and F9.
2. **State four to six testable program outcomes in CHARTER,** and add to each module's "By the end" one outcome about applying it to the reader's own situation. Outcomes must name capacities, not conclusions, or they breach "Not converging on a moral framework". *Cost:* short drafting, and a decision by Robert. Rests on F1 and F10.
3. **Add a short, plain signpost at the start of the README and Module 1** for anyone in such a situation now. It should say the program is slow and list the *kinds* of help (union, regulator, legal advice, confidential advice service, health), naming no body that has not been checked. *Cost:* about 150 words, written as orientation, not a process note (WORKFLOW). Rests on F5.
4. **Decide explicitly between narrowing the charter's reader and widening the cases.** Cases B and C are still owed (OPEN-QUESTIONS), so setting one outside white-collar work adds no case. Also add one prompt per module to transpose the case to the reader's own situation, with a caution about writing down real specifics. *Cost:* low; about 50 words per module, and a DECISIONS entry. Rests on F3, F4 and F8.
5. **Put one early pointer in Module 1 to where relief and remainder are taken up** (Module 8), so that a distressed reader knows it is coming. *Cost:* one sentence. Low confidence that it helps; the pilot would show. Rests on F7.
