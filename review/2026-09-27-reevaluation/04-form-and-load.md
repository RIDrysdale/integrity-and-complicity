# 04 — Form and load

## Summary

The form suits a reader working alone; the load and the missing record do not. (1) By my estimate, Modules 1–3 take about 6, 8 and 4 hours including reflection, so 45–55 hours for eight: feasible for a committed reader, uneven, and never stated. (2) Case first, disagreeing readings, a position taken before reading, and an action due by a date together supply much of a seminar, better than I predicted. (3) Comparing positions across modules is "part of the point", yet nothing gives the reader somewhere to keep one, and writing is optional throughout; Module 3's case itself warns that the remembered version keeps "improving". (4) Questions are many (39, 40, 25 "?") and unranked. (5) Likeliest stops: the library-only texts in Module 1, and Fried in Module 2. Recommended first: state the time, and add a one-page positions record.

## Question

Is the module form the right vehicle for a self-directed adult? The form is a case, three or four readings chosen to disagree, five-part commentary on each, a return to the case, and a list of what was left unsettled. How much does it ask of the reader?

## First principles

*Written before any program file was opened. I shortened it for length after the comparison. The predictions and options are unchanged.*

**Practice.** The case method makes the learner commit to a view before theory arrives. Its force comes from a facilitator and from peers who disagree, and alone a case risks being read as a story. The seminar and Great Books model keeps its apparatus thin because the conversation does the work, so a solo version has to replace the seminar, not only the reading list. Executive ethics teaching is short (one case, one to three hours) and leaves a few portable questions. Adult-learning principles say adults learn from problems they already have and need to see their own progress. **Unverified** as empirical findings.

**Self-study.** Self-paced courses complete poorly. Reich and Ruipérez-Valiente (*Science*, 2019, "The MOOC pivot") report about 3% completion across MIT and Harvard edX courses in 2017–18. I checked this through search summaries, not the paper. It overstates failure among serious readers, but the direction holds. The effect sizes of cohorts, deadlines and commitment devices are **unverified** here. Reasoning from first principles:

- Unbounded sessions do more damage than difficulty.
- Without a teacher, nothing corrects a misreading.
- Without peers, the form must stage the disagreement itself.
- Readers need the cost before they start, and a stopping place that feels like an ending.
- Questions are cheap on the page and costly in the head. Ten per reading means none get answered. Two that are written down do more.

**Reading rate.** Brysbaert (*Journal of Memory and Language* 109, 2019) puts adult silent reading of non-fiction at about 238 words per minute (range 175–300), checked through search summaries. For difficult primary texts I plan on 70–150 words per minute, which is **unverified**.

**Predicted strengths.**
- The case gives motivation and a test bed.
- Readings that disagree stand in for the seminar.
- "What to watch for" and "What kind of argument this is" do a tutor's work.
- The return to the case gives closure.

**Predicted failures.**
- The load, perhaps 6–12 hours a module.
- The commentary coming to substitute for the texts.
- Questions that are read but never answered.
- No record of the reader's positions.
- No map for coming back after a break.
- Stopping at the first hard text that meets the first busy week.

**Options.**

| Option | Cost |
|---|---|
| Time estimate per module | very low |
| One-page map per module | low, about 250 words each |
| Prior-positions record | low; slight workbook feel |
| Core and optional depth | medium; weakens "chosen to disagree" |
| Fewer questions, flagged to be written | low; cuts text |
| Reading-group companion | medium, about 1,500 words a module |
| Worked example | medium; anchors the reader to its answer |
| Audio | high; premature |

**Primary-text estimates.** These come from memory, because the source sites were blocked by the egress proxy.
- Module 1: *Apology* about 12,000 words and *Crito* about 5,000 (both Jowett); *A Theory of Justice* §§18–19 about 4,500; Arendt about 11,000.
- Module 2: the Cicero passages about 5,000; the *Analects* and Mencius passages about 2,000; Montaigne III.10 (Cotton) about 12,000; Fried about 15,000.
- Module 3: the two Aquinas articles about 1,500; *Avodah Zarah* 6a–6b (Davidson) about 3,000; the four suttas about 3,000, most of it AN 6.63.

**Time-budget assumptions.**
- Commentary: 180 words per minute.
- Plato, Cicero and Montaigne: 150.
- Rawls, Arendt and Fried: 100.
- Aquinas, the Talmud, the Confucian texts and the suttas: 70–90, since the modules themselves say to re-read them.
- Reflection: 10 minutes to settle a position on the case, 3 minutes for each "Questions to carry" bullet, and 30 minutes for the closing.

Commentary, texts and reflection come to about 0.3, 4.5 and 1.4 hours (M1); 0.9, 4.9 and 2.1 (M2); 0.6, 1.8 and 1.4 (M3).

**Where the program agrees with first principles.**
- The case comes first, and the reader settles a position before reading.
- Each reading has a named opponent.
- "What kind of argument this is" is marked "read after the text", which is a tutor's sequencing move.
- "Returning to the case" gives a one-line prompt per reading, which works as a map.
- Each module ends with an action due by a date in the case: Monday, Thursday, the 12th. This is stronger than I predicted.
- "One reading per sitting" bounds each session.
- The retelling check (D-012; CHECKLIST §C) guards against the commentary replacing the texts.

**Where it departs.**
- No total time is given anywhere.
- There is no record of positions.
- The questions are unranked.
- There is no companion for groups, although the licence expects classroom use.

Making writing optional was a considered choice (D-005), but that entry gives no reason. Length is tracked only against the previous module (CHECKLIST §D), never against a reader's time. I found nothing in DECISIONS on reading alone versus in a group, or on keeping a record, so these departures look unexamined.

## Findings

1. **Evidence, high.** Module length varies 2.6×: 3,538, 9,293 and 6,788 words. The estimated load peaks at Module 2 (about 8 hours) and falls to about 4 hours in Module 3. The hours are estimates.
2. **Judgment, medium.** Eight modules at 45–55 hours are realistic for the charter's reader only as a commitment of several months, at about two sittings a week for five or six months. "One reading per sitting" turns Fried (about 3 hours with commentary) and Arendt (about 2 hours) into single sittings that no weeknight holds.
3. **Evidence, high.** No module and no README entry states a total time. Five of eleven readings get a number; the rest get "shorter still", "longest and hardest", "slower than that sounds", or nothing.
4. **Evidence, high.** README says "part of the point is to be able to compare what you said the first time with what you say later". The modules say "Hold onto this" (M1 l.221) and "Keep it alongside…" (M2 and M3 closings), yet writing is "not required. Nothing in this module is." No later module repeats the earlier options or questions. Module 3's case has the reader notice "the version you remember has been improving".
5. **Judgment, high.** That comparison therefore rests on memory, which is the failure Module 3 dramatises. This is the clearest mismatch between the form and a reader working alone.
6. **Judgment, medium-high.** Structurally, the form is well suited to a solo reader. What is lost is not the seminar's disagreement but the teacher's other jobs: correcting a misreading, saying which question matters, and keeping the reader going.
7. **Evidence, high. Judgment, medium.** The modules contain 39, 40 and 25 question marks, and 15, 28 and 15 "Questions to carry" bullets, many of them compound. None is marked as the one to answer. Alone, a reader will read most of them and answer few.
8. **Judgment, medium.** Measured against my estimated text lengths, commentary rises from about 0.1 of the primary text in Module 1 to about 0.9 in Module 3. On the *Analects* and Mencius (2,048 words of commentary) and on Aquinas (1,791), it probably equals or exceeds the text. Here a solo reader is most likely to read the commentary instead of the text.
9. **Judgment, medium.** Readers are most likely to stop at Module 1, Reading 3. That is the first library-only text, and D-004 records the cost. The other likely point is Module 2, Reading 4: Fried is the longest sitting in the longest module, at the point where novelty has worn off.
10. **Evidence, high.** CHARTER l.23 says "Written exercises are offered". The modules contain no exercises, only suggestions to write.
11. **Evidence, low.** D-012 records Module 2's "What to watch for" cut to 3,039 words. My count is 3,451, so either it has grown back or the counting method differs. Two stated times may be low if my estimates are right: Montaigne's "three quarters of an hour" implies about 270 words per minute, and the suttas' "twenty minutes" looks short if AN 6.63 runs to about 2,500 words.

## Recommendations

1. **State the time.** Add one line per module ("about six hours, in five sittings") and one in the README for the whole program, after an honest recount of the primary texts. *Cost:* very low. No change to the architecture.
2. **Positions record.** Add one page the reader can copy. For each module it would hold the case's options and three blanks: before, after, and the action by the date. Each later module's closing would quote back the earlier module's options. Writing stays optional, but the comparison gets somewhere to live. *Cost:* low, about 300 words. It touches the charter's framing of "reading and reflection", so it goes to Robert.
3. **Flag one question per reading, or three per module,** as the ones to answer if the reader answers nothing else. *Cost:* low. It ranks questions, not readings, so WORKFLOW's no-pre-judging rule is not breached.
4. **Add a time and ratio check to CHECKLIST §D.** Measure against a reader's hours, not only against the previous module. *Cost:* very low.
5. **Run a timed test of Module 1 or 2 on one outside reader** before Modules 4–8 are drafted. The charter itself says an error in the form "would be repeated across every module". *Cost:* medium, a few weeks. It changes D-006's timing, so it is Robert's call.
6. **Reading-group companion,** about one page a module. *Cost:* medium. Defer until after a reader test.

Not recommended for now:
- A core and optional-depth split, because dropping a reading removes one side of the disagreement.
- A worked example, because the reader would anchor on its answer.
- Audio.
