# Tensions

Disagreements between assigned readings that the program does not resolve. An entry appears here only once readings the reader actually encounters instantiate it. A disagreement between one reading and a plausible misuse of another does not qualify. Neither does a difference of route to the same conclusion, nor two texts answering different questions in different vocabularies. The test is that the readings, put to the same case, would say different things about it.

## T-001 — Refusal and what survives it

**Between:** Plato, *Apology*, and Plato, *Crito*. Module 1.

In the *Apology* Socrates refuses an official demand and treats the danger to himself as beside the point (32a–e). In the *Crito* he refuses to escape a sentence he regards as unjust, and the Laws' speech argues that obligations to the city persist even after it has wronged him (50a–54d).

**Why it matters in the report case.** A manager's demand can be refused while obligations to colleagues remain in force. The reverse also holds: having obligations to colleagues does not by itself make producing the report permissible. Each inference needs its own argument, and neither reading supplies both.

**A reconciliation worth testing, not the entry's answer.** Distinguish being required to *do* wrong from being required to *suffer* wrong. Socrates refuses the first and accepts the second. The difficulty is that the Laws appear to demand more than this: the argument from tacit agreement claims that staying constitutes consent to the terms, which reaches past suffering a wrong into endorsing the arrangement that produced it.

**Left open.** Which obligations survive the rejection of a particular demand, and why. What would have to be true for continued membership to count as agreement. Whether the two dialogues genuinely disagree, or answer different questions asked in different circumstances, or give one answer twice.

The first set Cicero's duty of disclosure against Fried's location of the correction in the rules. It could not be shown that the two would decide the same case differently, which is what the rule above requires: Cicero offers a formulation and Fried denies that any formulation settles every case, and those are not contrary answers. The manager's report is also commissioned, produced and scheduled, where the merchant's cargo is his own — a difference that may decide the case before the merchant's question arises.

The second set Mencius 4B.3 against Fried on whether how you are treated bears on what you owe. Fried's footnote 35 defeats it: the professional rules he relies on permit withdrawal where a client's own conduct makes representation unreasonably difficult, so the client's behaviour is among his conditions after all.

The clearest instantiated disagreement in Module 2 is between Antipater and Diogenes over the merchant at Rhodes — contrary answers, on identical facts, in a text the reader is assigned. It is not recorded here because this file is for disagreements between readings, and both men are inside one of them. Whether that scope should widen is an open question rather than an oversight.
