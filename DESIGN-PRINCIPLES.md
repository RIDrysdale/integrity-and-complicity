# Design Principles

The rules every part of the program is held to.

### 1. Every reading earns its place

A text is included because it materially improves the reader's ability to think about the central question, never because it is canonical. Aristotle would be here because habituation explains what repeated compromise does to character, not because Aristotle is important. If we cannot say why a reading belongs, it does not belong, however famous it is.

Two questions are put to every candidate:

1. Does this make the reader better able to reason about conduct inside compromised institutions, or is it merely intellectually interesting?
2. Does this introduce a genuinely useful moral perspective, or is it here to make the bibliography look broader?

If the answer to either is the latter, it is cut.

### 2. Tensions are preserved, not resolved

Readings are chosen to conflict, and the syllabus does not converge on a single moral framework. The architecture may anticipate a tension; the syllabus must earn it. A conflict is recorded in `notes/tensions.md` only once readings the reader actually encounters instantiate it. Anticipated conflicts that no assigned reading supports are a wishlist.

### 3. The reader is never presumed to be the hero, and self-scrutiny cuts both ways

The reader is as likely to be partly implicated, dependent, frightened, uncertain, benefiting from the arrangement they criticize, or simply wrong. Every module asks some version of both questions:

- *How might I be wrong to object?* Am I describing wrongdoing, incompetence, disagreement, or conduct I merely dislike? What facts might I lack? What incentive do I have to see myself as the principled party?
- *How might my uncertainty be a way of avoiding a cost?* Is my demand for more information, more nuance, more time, the mechanism by which I avoid acting?

These are opposing failure modes. The reader should become suspicious of both. The questions rotate between modules so the practice does not harden into ritual.

### 4. Moral uncertainty does not suspend agency

"I don't know" is a legitimate judgment and not a resting place. Inside an institution, not deciding is a decision. The program repeatedly asks what responsible action looks like when certainty is unavailable. This belongs in every module; it is not a topic to be handled once.

### 5. Not everything is gray

Ambiguity is not the program's aesthetic. Some institutions are corrupt and some compromises are rotten. The syllabus includes cases where the right answer is reasonably clear and the difficulty lies entirely in the cost of acting on it. The reader should be able to tell *gray* from *merely difficult*.

### 6. Theory is taught retrospectively

The reader meets the problem, then the text, then a short note on what kind of argument they have just encountered. By the end they should understand the major theoretical approaches without having been marched through a taxonomy.

### 7. Cases move from ordinary to extreme

Most cases are mundane: reports, metrics, colleagues, policies, budgets. Historical extremes remain, because they expose principles with unusual clarity, but they do not dominate. Anchor cases recur and escalate across modules so the reader can watch their own judgment shift and watch a boundary appear. The program includes cases where the dissenter was wrong and cases where someone who stayed was right.

### 8. Other traditions enter where they have most to say

Readings from outside the Western canon are not included as analogues to Western positions, are not distributed for coverage, and are not required in every module. A tradition is placed where it has real purchase on a specific problem, and where it is placed it carries a full reading's weight.

In practice:

- We name specific thinkers, texts, nations, and traditions. We never write about "the Indigenous view," "the African view," or "the Eastern view."
- Religious frameworks are presented with their premises intact, not secularized to sit comfortably beside modern philosophy. The reader need not share the premises to learn from the structure of the argument.
- For each such reading we ask: *does this offer another answer to our question, or does it show we have been asking the question wrongly?* The second kind justifies revising the architecture, which is why `ARCHITECTURE.md` stays provisional.
- A tradition represented only by a pointer is not represented.
- The test is not how many traditions appear. It is whether any of them was allowed to change something. A program in which every unfamiliar reading confirms a conclusion already reached has used those readings as decoration.

This is a requirement on the finished program, not on each module.

### 9. A reading the reader cannot obtain is not a reading

Availability is a selection criterion, not a convenience. Prefer texts that are freely and durably accessible and that carry edition-independent locators: Stephanus numbers, Bekker numbers, sections, chapters, verses. Page numbers are not used.

Where an in-copyright work has no adequate substitute, it may be assigned with its limitation stated plainly and nothing quoted from an unreliable copy. Where an argument is worth knowing but the text is not worth assigning, it becomes a pointer under the rule in `WORKFLOW.md`.

Nothing is cited that has not been checked. A missing locator is a normal scholarly limitation; an invented one is a fabrication.

### 10. Robert holds final editorial authority
