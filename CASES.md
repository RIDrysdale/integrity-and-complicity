# Anchor cases

Cases recur across modules and escalate (design principle 7). The reader takes a position before reading and returns to it afterwards, so they can watch their own judgment move.

**Status:** Case A is written. Its escalations are drafted and not yet placed in modules. Cases B and C are placeholders.

## Case A — The internal report

### A.1 (Module 1)

You are responsible for preparing an internal report. Your manager asks you to remove or soften several unfavorable findings because, in their view, the report is technically accurate but gives senior leadership an unnecessarily negative impression of the team's performance. Nothing you are being asked to write is literally false. You believe the revised report would nevertheless leave a reasonable reader with a materially misleading impression.

Your manager argues that every department presents its work favorably, that leadership already understands how these reports are produced, that your team's budget could be harmed by unnecessary candor, and that several colleagues' jobs may eventually depend on that budget.

You are not being asked to break a law or falsify a number. What do you do?

**Take a position.** Comply. Refuse. Negotiate a middle path. Leave, or begin planning to. I don't know yet (then: what would you actually do on Monday?). Information is missing (then: what, and could you get it?).

### Escalations (drafted, unplaced)

- **A.2 — It worked.** The softened report went up. Nothing bad happened, the budget held, and your manager thanked you. You are asked again, for the next quarter. Does the first compliance change the second question? Candidate: Module 8, habituation.
- **A.3 — It was used.** A decision was taken partly on the strength of your report, and someone outside your team was harmed by it. You learn this a year later. What, if anything, do you now owe? Candidate: Module 3 or E3.
- **A.4 — You are the manager.** You now hold the budget, and a junior analyst brings you a report that is accurate and damaging. You find yourself making your old manager's argument, and it is better than you remembered. Candidate: Module 2 or 5.
- **A.5 — The soft version is the norm.** Every department does it. Yours is the only honest report in the cycle, and it reads as incompetence rather than candour. Candidate: Module 5, systems.
