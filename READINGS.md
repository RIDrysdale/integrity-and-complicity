Modules 1 and 2 each carry the cap of two pointers. Anything further in either must be assigned or dropped.

All four of Module 2's readings are freely obtainable. Locators for the Confucian passages were confirmed against ctext.org and, for the Mencius chapter numbers, against Legge's printed numbering; Eno's translation was checked to use the same numbering. Fried's article has no locator system of its own beyond its numbered sections and lettered subsections, which is what the module cites.

The Cicero assignment is III.43–58, extended from III.49–58 after a later check (D-017). The Latin was checked at the Latin Library and Miller's English at LacusCurtius.

All three of Module 3's readings are free and none requires a library. The *Summa* is the 1920 English Dominican translation at New Advent, cited by question and article. The Talmud is the William Davidson translation at Sefaria under CC BY-NC, cited by tractate and folio. The suttas are Bhikkhu Sujato's translations at SuttaCentral, published free of copyright, cited by collection and sutta number, which hold across every translation. Each was opened and read in full.

Two locators were omitted rather than guessed. The nine-item list at q. 62 a. 7 circulates as a Latin mnemonic verse; the English is confirmed and the Latin is not, so the module uses the English. *Mesayea lidei ovrei aveira*, the weaker category covering help the other party did not need, comes from Tosafot on *Avodah Zarah* 6b and is not in the Gemara; no English text of it has been checked and the module does not rely on it.

Module 3's fourth slot stands empty. Havel was dropped for want of a text that can be quoted; La Boétie's *Discourse on Voluntary Servitude* is the nearest free candidate and remains available if a reader pass finds the module short of a text that declines to do the accounting at all (D-020).

Other works considered and dropped are in DECISIONS.md: Applbaum for Module 2 (D-008).

## Traditions outside the Western canon

Placed where a tradition has real purchase on a specific problem, not distributed for coverage (design principle 8). The Confucian entry is assigned in Module 2, the Pali entry in Module 3, and the *Gita* is unplaced.

| Work | Locator system | Access | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Bhagavad Gita* | chapter and verse | Free, many translations | Arjuna refuses to act because his role requires something he finds monstrous, and the answer he receives concerns duty, detachment from outcome, and finally the setting aside of duty altogether. Drafted for Module 2 and withdrawn (D-014). Proposed for Module 7, on what may be demanded of someone who cannot cleanly withdraw, to be earned when that module is built. |
| Confucius, *Analects*; Mencius | book and chapter | Free | These texts characteristically ask what makes someone a good ruler, minister, parent or child, rather than opening from a conflict between personal conscience and office. That changes where an inquiry starts. It does not show that obligations cannot be stated without reference to roles: Mencius 2A.6 moves from a capacity every person has to how rulers should govern, and is assigned because it tells against the stronger reading the module once took. The remonstrance material — how a subordinate corrects a superior, and when to withdraw from office — bears on Modules 4 and 6 and is largely left for them; *Analects* 4.26 is reserved for Module 4. Module 2 takes 4.18, whose two defensible translations disagree about whether remonstrance may continue after it is refused, and 13.15, which concerns what a ruler needs rather than how a subordinate should speak. |
| *Aṅguttara Nikāya* (Pali Canon) | collection and sutta number | Free (SuttaCentral) | AN 4.264 names four ways of being in a killing — doing it, encouraging it, approving it, praising it — and sorts them by where they leave the agent rather than by what the victim lost, which is the reverse of the principle organising Aquinas's nine. No victim appears in it, nothing is apportioned, and whether the killing came off is not asked. AN 5.177 forbids five trades outright, prospectively and without regard to outcome. Assigned in Module 3 on design principle 1. Not offered as principle 8's instance. |

All three have the access profile design principle 9 asks for: free, durable, and numbered by verse, chapter or sutta rather than by page.

Where design principle 8 stands is recorded in ARCHITECTURE.md, under What is not yet decided. Why neither the Confucian nor the Pali reading counts as its instance, and why the strongest candidate for Module 3 could not be assigned, is in D-010, D-018 and D-021. What withdrawing the *Gita* cost is in D-014.

## Gaps

Module 3's slate is set. No candidates yet for Modules 4 through 8.

Two of Module 1's four assigned readings are in copyright and require a library. That is acceptable for readings with no adequate free substitute, but a program in which it is the norm would fail design principle 9. Later modules should do better, not worse. Module 2 does: all four of its readings are free.

Weber's "Politics as a Vocation" is a pointer and not an assignment for one reason: there is no English translation that is both reliable and freely available. If a sound free translation appears it should be reconsidered as an assigned reading (D-009).
