# Architecture

**Status: provisional.** This document maps the problems the program addresses and the order in which it addresses them. It assigns no readings. It will be revised when a reading shows that a problem has been framed wrongly, which design principle 8 anticipates and permits.

## How this document is meant to be used

The map is deliberately larger than the commitment.

- **Core path (1–8).** The eight modules that together constitute a complete program. If nothing beyond these is ever built, the program is finished, not abandoned.
- **Expansion (E1–E3).** Real modules, worth building, not required for completion.

Progress is measured against the core path. Building an expansion module is an addition, never the settling of a debt. The distinction exists so that progress can be registered at all: a program with unbounded scope is never any nearer to finished.

## Organizing logic

Modules are organized around **problems a person actually faces**, not around philosophers and not around a taxonomy of ethical theories. The sequence follows the arc of a situation rather than the structure of a discipline: what binds me, what I owe, what I am part of, what I am not saying, where responsibility went, what I can do, what it will cost, and what I have to live with afterwards.

Two things deliberately do **not** appear as modules:

- **Judgment under uncertainty.** Design principles 3 and 4 put it inside every module. A module of its own would let the other seven treat uncertainty as somebody else's problem.
- **Authority, law, and obedience** as a standalone unit. It is inside Module 1, where the readings already raise it. Freestanding, it drags the program toward the civil-disobedience course the charter rules out.

## Core path

### 1. Conscience and Obligation
*What makes an institutional demand binding, and what could justify refusing one?*

The entry point. A demand arrives that you believe is wrong but that is not illegal and not obviously outrageous. The module establishes the two poles the rest of the program works between: grounds for refusal, and obligations that survive being wronged.

### 2. Role, Loyalty, and What You Owe
*Do the obligations of a job, a profession, or a membership differ in kind from ordinary moral obligations?*

The defense that does most of the real work in institutional life: *this is my role, these are my people, this is not my call.* The module has to take that seriously before it can be examined, because it is frequently correct.

This question was once restated as *what does a role make you owe — and is there a you apart from the roles?*, on the ground that the Confucian material denies there are two separable things to compare. It has been reverted. The ground was overstated, and the original wording never presupposed two selves in the first place — *they are inseparable* was always an available answer to it. The replacement also invited a question about personal identity that the module's readings cannot develop.

### 3. Complicity and Participation
*What counts as taking part in wrongdoing someone else is doing?*

The program's title concept and the point where distinctions have to get sharp: doing, ordering, enabling, facilitating, benefiting, staying silent, staying. These are not degrees along one line, and treating them as one is a common error.

### 4. Silence, Speech, and Truth
*When does saying nothing become a moral act?*

Placed here because silence is the most common real response and the least examined. Most people never refuse and never comply; they say nothing in the meeting and something afterward in the corridor.

### 5. Responsibility Inside Systems
*Does responsibility divided among many people leave less for each?*

Division of labor, procedural distance, the many-hands problem. Also the strongest challenge to the program's own framing: that focusing on individual conduct inside a bad system is itself a way of leaving the system unexamined. The module must let that challenge land.

### 6. Refusal, Dissent, and Exit
*What can actually be done, and how do the options compete?*

Treated as one module rather than three because in a real moment they are not separate topics — they are rival uses of the same occasion. Protest, internal escalation, slow obstruction, public disclosure, quiet non-cooperation, resignation, staying to repair. Exit is not assumed to be the clean option.

### 7. Compromise, Cost, and Survival
*How much may morality demand of someone who cannot afford to lose?*

Dependence, dependents, fear, and the difference between prudence and cowardice. Includes the case the program owes its reader: where the right course is reasonably clear and the whole difficulty is what it costs.

### 8. Integrity and What You Live With
*What is it to remain one person over time, and what remains after a choice that could not be made cleanly?*

Coherence, self-deception, habituation, moral remainder, regret, and self-forgiveness. Closes the program where it began: not with a verdict on the anchor case, but with the reader's account of their own judgment and how it changed.

## Expansion

### E1. Institutions Worth Belonging To
*What makes an institution deserve loyalty, and what does repairing one require?*

The program's implied counterweight. Without it, the reader may finish with no account of why anyone should stay.

### E2. Law, State, and Citizenship
The political case at full strength, for readers who want it. Kept out of the core so it cannot colonize the workplace material.

### E3. Repair, Reconciliation, and the Wronged
Responsibility looking outward rather than inward: what is owed to the people harmed. Distinct from Module 8, which concerns the agent's own life.

## What is not yet decided

- No readings are assigned in this document, including for Module 1.
- The eight problems are Western-analytic in their carving: discrete problems, rival positions, an individual agent deciding. Design principle 8 does not require every module to test that carving, but it does require that somewhere in the program a tradition be allowed to reject it rather than supply another position within it. Module 2 tested this and the claim did not hold. Its Confucian readings change where inquiry starts — they ask what makes someone a good minister or parent rather than opening from a conflict between conscience and office — but that is a difference of emphasis and method, not a rejection of the framing, and the module's question has been reverted accordingly. Nothing in the program yet counts as an instance.
- Anchor cases and their escalation across modules are drafted in `CASES.md`.

## Coverage of the charter's concerns

The charter names what the program is about: integrity, complicity, compromise, loyalty, dissent, courage, prudence, responsibility, and knowing when accommodation becomes participation. Each lands somewhere in the core path — integrity in 8, complicity in 3, compromise and courage and prudence in 7, loyalty in 2, dissent in 6, responsibility in 5, and accommodation shading into participation across 3 and 4. Module 4 is the one not named in that list, and it is there because silence is the most common response and the least examined.
